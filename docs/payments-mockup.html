Pay first, then it is released

1. When the contract is signed

Same rules as visits. The one-time origination fee is set when you send the contract (for example $100).

  1. Day 0Tue Oct 6You countersign, so both signatures are in

    The agreement is complete and in effect. The customer is emailed an invoice for the origination fee with a Pay button. They do not receive the signed agreement yet: it stays locked in their portal and is not emailed.

  2. Paid + 3business daysSigned copy is released if payment still stands

    Customer can view and download the signed PDF, and gets an email saying so.

  3. UnpaidGoes to the unpaid sequence in section 3

    Nothing is released while it is unpaid.

  4. WaivedYou can waive any fee, any time your button

    The invoice is marked Waived, reminders stop, and whatever was locked is released right away with no 3-day hold. Each waiver is logged.

2. After each visit

Day 0 is the day you enter the report and the invoice. Weekends and federal holidays do not count toward the 3 business days.

  1. Day 0Tue Oct 20You enter the visit and the invoice amount

    Report is saved but locked. Customer gets an email with a Pay button. Nobody else receives anything.

  2. Paidany dayPayment received Stripe tells us

    Customer is emailed a receipt. The 3 business day hold starts.

  3. Paid + 3business daysReport is released if payment still stands

    Customer sees it in the portal and gets the report email, and the county copy goes out at the same time. If the payment was refunded or disputed during the hold, release stops and you are alerted.

  4. UnpaidGoes to the unpaid sequence in section 3

    The report stays locked for the customer and the county.

3. If an invoice goes unpaid (contract or visit)

Follows your agreement: a bill unpaid 30 days is cause (3(d)(1)), then 30 days written notice to the owner and the health department before it ends. Paying at any point before the end stops everything and the agreement continues.

  1. Day 0Invoice emailed

    Locked item stays locked.

  2. Day 1 to 7Daily reminder email 7 emails

    Same short message with the Pay button. Stops when payment arrives.

  3. Day 15One more reminder, plainly worded

    States the due date (day 30) and what happens after it.

  4. Day 30unpaidNotice of intent to terminate, ready for your OK your OK

    You get an email and an admin alert. The system drafts the notice to the owner and the copy to the county health department, both with the termination date (day 60) and a printable letter. Your agreement requires written notice delivered in person or by certified mail, so you mail it and enter the tracking number. The email goes out when you click.

  5. Day 31 to 60Owner can still pay

    A weekly reminder, and the portal banner shows the termination date. Payment before day 60 keeps the agreement in effect.

  6. Day 60still unpaidAgreement cancelled, county told your OK

    Drafted for your one-click OK. The contract is marked cancelled, and the county is told the system has no operation and maintenance contract.

Settled

  • $100 origination fee at signing; the signed copy stays locked until it is paid and the 3 business day hold passes.
  • Visit invoices: $250 standard; you can waive any fee.
  • Unpaid sequence follows the agreement: 30 days, then 30 days notice, with your OK before anything goes to the owner or county.
  • The county's report copy also waits for payment.

Still needed

  1. Certified mail. Section 9 requires notices to be delivered in person or by certified or registered mail. The system can make the printable letter and record the tracking number, but you have to mail it. An email alone may not count as notice.
  2. The county copy waiting on payment. Section 1(c) has you report inspections on a fixed schedule, so a late report can put you out of step with it. The system will log why each report was held, so you have a record, but this is a risk you are choosing.
  3. The agreement wording. Section 2(a) names a yearly fee, which no longer matches your billing, and section 1(e) has you doing repairs. A lawyer should fix both.
  4. Stripe account for Overwatch Septic (about 2.9% plus 30 cents per card payment). I would set it up for you to approve; I never see card numbers.
  5. County email address for each county you serve, and your real business mailing address for the letters.
